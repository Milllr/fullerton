# Fullerton Productions site

Portfolio for James Downey (Miller's dad), Lighting Designer, Fullerton Productions, Toronto. Vite + vanilla TypeScript, no framework.

- Miller wants the drawings to look exactly like Vectorworks (industry people must recognise it). Chosen: Hidden Line sets + Spotlight drafting, black background with white lines, Vectorworks defaults with placeholder labels. See README, The drawings. Perspective sheets are NTS (no scale bar or dimension: an LD would catch it). Drawing code: src/engine/drafting.ts, beamcad.ts, fixture.ts; trace cleanup tools/hiddenline.py.
- Concept: dark stage, every show is a sheet with its set traced from photos; a rig hangs off the truss under the nav: Spot L (1) and Spot R (2) moving heads at the wings, Panel L (3) and Panel R (4) soft panels inside pointing straight down, Hazer (5). Light reveals the real photo.
- Behaviour Miller chose (2026-10-22): Exec 204 Fill is at 72% from load on every device (the panels come up as they draw in during the opening), and the spots rest at 15% everywhere (`desk.spotDim`), soft accents over the flood. Desktop, both spots converge on the cursor; there is no automatic idle sweep any more (it swept beams across text people were reading; Exec 203 still works when raised). Touch screens, spots cross on the show in the middle of the screen (Spot L lands right of centre, Spot R left) and roam slowly; a finger drag pulls both spots to it, back to roaming 1.2 s after release.
- Controls: a pull-up desk modelled faithfully on grandMA3 (src/desk/): programmer with 3-stage Clear, feature groups, encoders, executors, preset pools, real MA command syntax. Miller wants it fully industrial and physical, and judged harshly against real MA3 (taste is everything for James; nothing may break). The screen follows the grandMA3 manual (fixture sheet, Dynamic pool, sequence sheet, encoder bar with feature group control bar and on-screen Grand Master, command line at the bottom as `James[Fixture]>`); see README, The desk. Never reproduce the MA logo or wordmark: a neutral glyph stands in title fields.
- The console is grandMA3 too: Miller A/B tested MA3 screens on our hardware (A) against full MA3 (B) and chose B on 2026-10-16; the switch and A are gone. Backlit keys (legend lights via `is-lit`, no LED dots; the header keys match), dual encoders with lights, letterbox executor labels, no B.O., Goto or Grand Master fader (GM on screen, `Black` command). Executors are 201 to 204 and the key is Go+; the line under the command line echoes what ran in desk syntax (errors red). Styles hang off `.desk.ma3`.
- Miller picked version A: the desk and header chrome are industrial, the rest of the site stays quiet and minimal. Version B (whole site industrial) was dropped. (That earlier A/B was about site chrome, not the desk console.)
- Light should leak across the scene naturally (physical, subtle): hot centre with field fall-off, throw fall-off, faint photo spill past the pool, gobo floor pool, colour bounce, shaft scatter, desk-lip spill, crossing pools screen rather than add. See README, How the light behaves.
- Headless Chromium here renders at about 1 fps; use `?fast` (dev only) and `window.__rig` (dev only) for tests. Use the `.ts` style helpers in src/engine/css.ts (setVar, setText) for per-frame DOM writes.
- The URL hash only ever holds an open show; in-page links scroll without touching it (see README, Browsers and devices).
- Motion rule from James's lighting doctrine: nothing snaps. Pan/tilt use a damped spring with capped speed; idle sweep is 24 s across.
- Copy is deliberately minimal: names, networks, years. No bio. No dashes (en or em) in copy.
